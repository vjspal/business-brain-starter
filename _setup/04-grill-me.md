# Stage 3 — Fill the vault

Get what they know out of their head and into notes. They talk, you write.

This works because most of what makes a business run is knowledge the owner would never think to write down — it only surfaces when someone asks a specific question. You are that someone. Be a sharp interviewer, not a form.

**This is the stage that matters.** Everything before it was scaffolding.

## One domain per session

Never interview across multiple domains at once — it produces shallow notes in all of them. Pick one, go deep, write the notes, stop.

Suggested order, though follow their energy:

1. **Sales** — objections and scripts. Usually the richest material and the most immediately useful.
2. **Operations** — how work actually gets done.
3. **Marketing** — what brings people in, what's worked, what flopped.
4. **Delivery domain** (clients / jobs / products) — the specifics.
5. **Systems** — tools and automations.
6. **Knowledge** — lessons, post-mortems, things learned the hard way.

Aim for 10–15 questions per session, then write. If they're in flow, keep going. When answers get short, stop — that's fatigue, and tired answers make thin notes.

## The rules

**One question at a time. Never a list.** Twenty questions at once gets zero answered. One good question gets a paragraph. This matters more than anything else here.

**Ask for specifics, never summaries.** "What's your sales process?" produces a useless abstraction. "Someone just told you your quote is double what they expected — what are the first words out of your mouth?" produces a note worth having.

**Chase vagueness.** Abstract, hedged, or "you know, the usual" — don't move on:
- "Give me the last time that actually happened."
- "What would you literally say? Words, not the gist."
- "Depends on what? Give me both branches."

**Capture exact words.** Anything they'd say out loud goes in verbatim, in a quote block.

**Ask what doesn't work.** Failures, customers they now refuse, angles that flopped, the mistake they only made once. Negative knowledge is rarer and more useful than positive knowledge, and almost nobody writes it down.

**Follow the interesting thread over the planned one.** An offhand mention of a job that went wrong is worth more than the next three questions on your list.

**Don't lead.** Never put the answer in the question.

**Don't compliment.** Acknowledge briefly, move on.

## Writing as you go

Write notes every 3–4 answers rather than batching to the end.

- One idea per note
- Answer first, detail after
- Descriptive title
- Self-contained
- Their words in quote blocks
- `[[Link]]` to related notes — check what exists first so you link rather than duplicate
- Set `confidence` honestly
- `ai_index: false` on anything sensitive

**Never invent detail to make a note look finished.** If they didn't say it, it isn't in the note. Add `## Open questions` for the gaps — that becomes the next session's agenda.

## Keep the raw answers

Append the questions and their unedited answers to `70_Knowledge/interviews/YYYY-MM-DD-<domain>.md`. Raw answers usually contain more than you extracted, and re-reading them later surfaces notes you missed.

## Ending a session

Report briefly:

- Notes created, with paths
- The one or two most valuable things that came out
- What's still missing in this domain
- Which domain to do next, and why

Then ask whether to continue or stop. Don't roll into another domain unprompted — this is tiring work and stopping at a good point is better than pushing to a bad one.

## When to stop entirely

Around 40–60 notes the vault becomes genuinely useful. It will never be finished — a business brain grows for as long as the business does. Move to Stage 4 and test it once there's enough to test.
